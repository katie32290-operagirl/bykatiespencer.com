Company History

From Lake George
to Saratoga Springs

July 5, 1962. The Diamond Point Theatre on Lake George. An audience of 230.

That evening, Lake George Opera raised the curtain on its first production, Johann Strauss’s Die Fledermaus.

The company was the idea of Fred Patrick and his wife, soprano Jeanette Scovotti. They wanted a permanent summer repertory company that sang opera in English and gave talented young American singers a stage. Their first season set the pace: 46 fully staged performances in eight weeks, accompanied by two pianists.

Chapter 1

1962–1980

The Lake
George Years

Audiences grew quickly, and an orchestra soon replaced the two pianos. In 1965 the company moved into the newly completed auditorium at Queensbury High School in Glens Falls and formed its first Board of Directors. The same year it lost its founder: Fred Patrick died of cancer at 37.

David Lloyd, then artistic director, became general director and led the company until 1980. Under Lloyd, Lake George Opera staged its first contemporary and American operas, Menotti’s The Telephone in 1965 and Robert Ward’s The Crucible in 1966.

New work became part of the company’s identity. Lloyd’s tenure brought four world-premiere productions: David Amram’s Twelfth Night and Robert Baksa’s Aria da Capo, both in 1968, Jose Bernardo’s The Child in 1974, and Alva Henderson’s The Last of the Mohicans in 1977.

He also looked past the stage, introducing Opera-on-the-Lake in 1972 and forming the Contemporary American Opera Studio in 1980.

Chapter 2

1981–1997

Opera Beyond
the Mainstage

Paulette Haupt-Nolen, artistic director from 1981 through 1985, brought in new partners: the Eugene O’Neill Theater Center, to workshop new operas, and Proctor’s Theatre, for a production of Man of La Mancha.

She also produced the company’s first opera at the Spa Little Theater in Saratoga Springs, the world premiere of Glenn Paxton’s The Adventures of Friar Tuck, and in 1985 sent opera on the road with the traveling Opera-On-Wheels program.

Artistic directors Brian Lingham (1986–87) and John Balme (1988–91) followed. The Opera-to-Go education program began in 1986, and the company gave the world premiere of Mark Houston’s Hazel Kirke, an opera set in the Hudson River Valley in the 1840s. While the Queensbury auditorium was renovated, the 1989 and 1990 seasons played at Adirondack Community College.

In 1991 Susan T. Danis became the company’s first managing director, a newly created post, and the company returned to Queensbury. 1993 was a transitional year: David Lloyd came back as interim artistic director, and the company presented three gala concerts featuring many alumni, with no fully staged productions.

Joseph Illick then led five summers of wide-ranging programming, including Rossini’s La Donna del Lago, Massenet’s Cendrillon, Jorge Martin’s Tobermory, Richard Wargo’s The Music Shop, and Britten’s A Midsummer Night’s Dream, with the Boys Choir of Harlem as the Spirits in the Forest.

Chapter 3

1998–2013

A Home in
Saratoga Springs

In 1998, renovations at Queensbury High School sent the summer season to the 500-seat Spa Little Theater in Spa State Park. The move was meant to be temporary, but the theater proved ideal for intimate opera and became the company’s main stage. That same year the company began performing operas in their original languages, with projected supertitles.

In 1999 conductor Daniel Beckwith and stage director Marc Verzatt became co-artistic directors, and William Florescu soon joined as artistic director. Together they defined the company’s brand of intimate opera theater, with Ariadne auf Naxos at the Spa Little Theater, Madama Butterfly with the Saratoga Performing Arts Center in its outdoor amphitheater, and, from 2000, a five-year Opera-to-Go touring cycle of operas by John Davies.

After taking on executive leadership in 2002, Florescu reintroduced American operas, featured Apprentice Artists in concerts with orchestra, started the Lake George Opera Summer Camp, and, with event chairs Ted and Carol Newlin, launched the annual Opera Ball.

When Florescu left for the Florentine Opera in Milwaukee in 2005, Curtis Tucker became the company’s eighth artistic director. His expanded 2006 season included the professional premiere of Ned Rorem’s Our Town and a semi-staged Apprentice Artist performance of Menotti’s The Medium. Over nine seasons, his tenure was known for acclaimed Gilbert and Sullivan.

In 2011 the company celebrated its 50th season and formally became Opera Saratoga, a name that reflects its home in the Saratoga Springs community.
